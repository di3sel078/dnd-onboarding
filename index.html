What Makes D&D Different?

Unlike a video game limited by code or a board game constrained by a board, D&D is bounded only by imagination. There is no single way to "win." Instead of competing against each other, players team up to overcome obstacles.

The biggest differentiator is the Dungeon Master/Game Master (DM/GM) who is a human referee and storyteller who reacts in real-time to the players' choices. If you want to try talking your way past a dragon instead of fighting it, or use a spell in a completely unconventional way, the DM adapts the world to your creativity on the fly.

The Core Loop of D&D

The DM describes the scene

→

The players state their action

↓

The dice decide the outcome

←

The DM narrates the results

↑ ↻ The loop repeats

Why People Love It

Ultimate Creative Freedom

You can build the exact character you want to be and attempt (almost) any action you can think of!

Real Connection

In an era of digital isolation, D&D requires sitting down (either virtually or physically) and actively engaging with friends for a few hours.

Unexpected Chaos

Because the dice ultimately decide the outcome of your actions, even the best-laid plans can fail hilariously, and desperate final attempts can succeed brilliantly. This creates unforgettable, completely unique moments.

Shared Ownership

By the end of the campaign, the players haven't just played a game, they've co-authored a fantasy novel that belongs exclusively to their group.

What Do I Need to Start?

  • Ideally, you want to have 3-5 players plus a Dungeon Master (DM)
    • Two players plus a DM works in a pinch, though it can feel sparse
    • Six or more players (plus a DM) is possible but it gets difficult to manage for DMs
  • A full set of dice or a dice rolling app
    • If your friends have dice you can always share!
  • A character sheet
  • A pencil (or something to edit your character sheet with, but not permanently!)

What Is a Character Sheet?

A character sheet is a single document that holds everything about your character in one place: your ability scores, race, class, skills, spells, equipment, and more. You'll fill it out as you make choices during character creation, and reference it constantly during play to look up your stats and abilities.
