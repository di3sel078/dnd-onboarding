A Few Definitions

Advantage & Disadvantage

Sometimes, something about your character (like their class or background), or the situation your character is in, will cause your DM to give you something called "advantage" or "disadvantage". When you have Advantage on a d20 roll, you roll the d20 twice and take the higher of the two numbers, then add any relevant modifiers. If you have Disadvantage, you roll the d20 twice and take the lower of the two numbers.

Natural 20's & Natural 1's

If you've heard of D&D before, you've probably heard of the excitement of rolling a "Natural 20", but what does that mean? Rules As Written (RAW), a Natural 20 only applies to a combat attack roll. When you roll a 20 (before "to hit" modifiers) on a d20 attack roll during combat, you may roll you damage dice twice and then add your damage modifier (more on attacking and damge in step 10). This is also referred to as a "Critical Hit".

A common house rule is to consider any Natural 20 (including outside of combat) a Critical Success. Another house rule is to consider Natural 1's as a "critical failure". So instead of picking a lock, maybe the rogue makes the lock even harder to pick by breaking an internal component. Natural 1's and Natural 20's (outside of combat) are not in the official rules, so make sure to check with your DM if they include them, before playing.

Note for DMs: Try to avoid making a Natural 1 completely humilitating, your players are still experts at their class.
